# Philosophy

> I am deeply curious about the big questions — what it means to think well, how to live deliberately, and what the great thinkers actually argued. Primary sources, not summaries.

## Core Questions
- What can I actually know, and how?
- How should I think about ethics and decisions under uncertainty?
- What does it mean to live without borrowed assumptions?
- How does philosophy of science shape how I evaluate claims?

## Essential Starting Points

- [ ] **Meditations — Marcus Aurelius** *(start here)*
  - Books 2–4: The core practice (impermanence, control, present action)
  - Books 5–6: Duty and the rational life
  - Book 9: On death and the shortness of all things

- [ ] **The Myth of Sisyphus — Camus**
  - Essay I: An Absurd Reasoning (the whole argument)
  - The Myth of Sisyphus (the final 10 pages)

- [ ] **Thus Spoke Zarathustra — Nietzsche**
  - Prologue: The Overman
  - "On the Three Metamorphoses"
  - "On Self-Overcoming"
  - "The Convalescent" (eternal recurrence)

- [ ] **The Structure of Scientific Revolutions — Kuhn**
  - Ch. 1–2: Normal science and paradigms
  - Ch. 6–8: Anomaly and crisis
  - Ch. 10: Revolutions as changes of world view

- [ ] **Critique of Pure Reason — Kant** *(selected)*
  - Preface to the 2nd Edition
  - Transcendental Aesthetic (space, time, intuition)
  - Introduction to the Transcendental Analytic
