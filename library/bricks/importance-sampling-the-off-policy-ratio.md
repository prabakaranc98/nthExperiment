# Importance Sampling & the Off-Policy Ratio

**One-liner:** Reweight samples drawn from a behavior distribution q to estimate an expectation under a target distribution p via the ratio w = p/q; in RL post-training the ratio r = π_new(a|s)/π_old(a|s) lets you reuse old rollouts to estimate the new policy's gradient.

## The formula / definition

Estimate E_{x∼p}[f(x)] using samples from q:

E_{x∼p}[f(x)] = E_{x∼q}[ (p(x)/q(x)) · f(x) ] ≈ (1/N) Σ_i w_i f(x_i),  w_i = p(x_i)/q(x_i)

Requires support coverage: q(x) > 0 wherever p(x)f(x) ≠ 0. The estimator is unbiased; variance blows up when p and q diverge (large w).

In policy gradients, the per-token (or per-action) ratio is:

r_t(θ) = π_θ(a_t | s_t) / π_{old}(a_t | s_t),   r appears in the surrogate L = E[ r_t · Â_t ]

So you collect rollouts under π_old, then take several gradient steps on π_θ reusing that data — turning on-policy PG into a sample-efficient near-off-policy update.

## Where it appears

- PPO — the clipped surrogate min(r_t Â_t, clip(r_t, 1±ε) Â_t) is exactly the off-policy ratio with clipping as a variance/trust-region surrogate.
- GRPO / RLVR — same r_t per token, advantage from group-normalized rewards instead of a value baseline; ratio enables multiple inner epochs per rollout batch.
- Off-policy / async RL (speculative-async rollout, replay) — IS corrects for stale rollouts generated by a lagged policy or a different sampler (vLLM) than the trainer.
- Self-normalized IS (SNIS) — divide by Σ w_i instead of N: biased but lower variance, used when normalizers are unknown.

## Common mistake

Believing the clipping in PPO/GRPO makes the objective unbiased or a proper IS estimator — it does not. Clipping deliberately *biases* the gradient to bound the ratio and control variance/trust region; it is a heuristic surrogate, not a corrected estimator. Also: forgetting the ratio is only valid one or a few steps off-policy — after π_θ drifts far from π_old, w explodes and the estimate becomes useless without re-sampling.
