# Craft — Technical Writing

**Type:** Craft (Production Function)
**Serves:** Makes everything else legible. Closes the shipping gap between understanding and communication. The Olah-grade depth + Karpathy-grade cadence model.

---

## The Model

Two writers define the target:

**Chris Olah** — depth without compromise. His posts on circuits, features, and attention are the standard for technical writing that does not lie, does not simplify into falsehood, and trusts the reader to follow. The diagrams do real work. The prose is precise. Nothing is there for atmosphere.

**Andrej Karpathy** — cadence and velocity. Ships often. Writes the thing before it's perfect. Nanograd, the GPT post, the unreasonable effectiveness of RNNs — shipped at pace, each one a complete, useful artifact. He does not wait until the understanding is complete; writing is part of the process of completing it.

The goal is to alternate: Olah-mode for the deep pieces that require diagrams and rigor, Karpathy-mode for the fast posts that ship understanding while it's fresh.

---

## What "From Scratch" Means Here
- Write one Karpathy-mode piece per experiment in the main curriculum: short, fast, honest about what you don't know
- Write one Olah-mode piece per subject per month: deep, visual, no shortcuts
- Study both writers technically: annotate their posts, identify their structural moves, identify where they make choices other writers would make differently

---

## The Two Modes

### Karpathy Mode (cadence)
- Target length: 500–1500 words
- Written while understanding is still fresh — within 48 hours of the experiment
- Permitted to be incomplete. Must be honest about the incompleteness.
- Structure: what I was trying to do → what I found → what I don't understand yet
- Ship it. Don't edit for a week.

### Olah Mode (depth)
- Target length: 2000–5000 words + diagrams
- Written after the experiment is done and reflected on
- Every claim must be justified. Every diagram must do work the prose cannot.
- Structure: the idea → why it's hard → how to think about it → the thing itself → what it connects to
- Do not ship until the structure is right. The structure is the argument.

## Build Progression

- [ ] **Step 1:** Read and annotate 3 Olah posts. For each: what is the skeleton? What do the diagrams accomplish that prose can't? Where does he make choices other writers would avoid?
  - Recommended: *Feature Visualization*, *Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits*, *Attention and Augmented RNNs*

- [ ] **Step 2:** Read and annotate 3 Karpathy posts. For each: what is the opening move? How does he handle uncertainty? What does he leave out and why?
  - Recommended: *The Unreasonable Effectiveness of RNNs*, *A Recipe for Training Neural Networks*, *Let's Build GPT*

- [ ] **Step 3:** Write your first Karpathy-mode post — about the first experiment you complete in the main curriculum. Ship it (even to a private gist).

- [ ] **Step 4:** Write your first Olah-mode post — pick one concept from the seminars and write it at depth with at least one diagram that does real explanatory work.

- [ ] **Step 5:** Develop a diagram vocabulary. What tools, what conventions, what visual language do you use consistently?
