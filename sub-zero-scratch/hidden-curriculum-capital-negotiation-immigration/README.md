# Hidden Curriculum — Capital, Negotiation & Immigration

**Type:** Background Operations
**Serves:** Converts the work into actual outcomes when offers land. Most people do the hard technical work and then leave 30–50% on the table because they don't understand equity, don't know how to negotiate with multiple offers, and don't have a clear O-1 strategy.

---

## Why This Exists

This is not in any curriculum. No one teaches it. It is learned through expensive mistakes, through friends who happen to know, or through paying a lawyer $500/hour for things you could have figured out yourself.

The goal here is to understand it before it matters — not while you're in the middle of an offer negotiation or a visa filing.

---

## Three Areas

### 1. Equity Compensation
Startups and large tech companies compensate differently. Understanding both is mandatory:

**Startups:**
- How to read a term sheet: preferred vs. common stock, liquidation preferences, anti-dilution
- Option grants: ISO vs. NSO, strike price, cliff + vesting, post-termination exercise windows (PTEP)
- Dilution: how future funding rounds affect your percentage
- The question no one asks: what is the liquidation preference stack, and where do I sit in it?

**Big Tech:**
- RSU mechanics: grant, vest, tax event at vest
- Refresh grants: how they work, how to negotiate them
- Total comp calculation: base + bonus + equity (at current price, not grant price)
- The 83(b) election: when it matters, what it costs to ignore it

### 2. Multi-Offer Negotiation
- How to create leverage without lying
- The timeline compression game: using one offer to accelerate others
- What is and isn't negotiable at each company tier (FAANG vs. growth startup vs. Series B)
- Competing offer mechanics: what to reveal, how to use it, when to stop
- The number you never say first — and what to do when they ask anyway

### 3. O-1A Visa Strategy
For people doing work that meets "extraordinary ability" criteria in science or research:

- What O-1A actually requires: the 8 criteria, how many you need, what "extraordinary" actually means in practice
- Evidence types that work: publications, citations, peer review, judging, high salary, media coverage
- Building the file proactively: what to document now, while you're doing the work
- Timeline: how long it takes, when to file, how to maintain status during the gap
- The employer sponsor question: who can sponsor, what they need, what to do if they won't
- EB-1A as the long game: how O-1A evidence becomes EB-1A evidence

## Build Progression

- [ ] **Step 1:** Read a Y Combinator Series A term sheet (publicly available). List every term you don't understand. Look up each one.

- [ ] **Step 2:** Model a startup equity grant. Assume: 0.1% at a $10M valuation, 4-year vest, 1-year cliff, two funding rounds (Series A at $50M, Series B at $200M), standard dilution. What is your stake worth at each outcome scenario?

- [ ] **Step 3:** Read Levels.fyi for a target company. Understand the full comp breakdown. Calculate what the equity component is worth at current stock price vs. grant-date price.

- [ ] **Step 4:** Write a negotiation script for a two-offer scenario. What do you say, in what order, to each company?

- [ ] **Step 5:** List every O-1A criterion. For each one, write down what evidence you currently have and what you could realistically build in 12 months.
